    Quadratic Forms and Optimization¶

    Quadratic forms¶

    For a symmetric matrix \(Q\), the expression \(\mathbf{x}^T Q \mathbf{x}\) is a single number, a quadratic form. It's the matrix version of \(qx^2\). Kinetic energy is one: for a single mass it's \(\tfrac{1}{2}mv^2\), and for a robot arm it's \(\tfrac{1}{2}\dot{\mathbf{q}}^T M \dot{\mathbf{q}}\).

    \(Q\) is positive definite if \(\mathbf{x}^T Q \mathbf{x} > 0\) for every nonzero \(\mathbf{x}\). Equivalently, all its eigenvalues are positive. Its graph is a bowl with a single lowest point. A matrix with eigenvalues of both signs gives a saddle instead. Along some directions a saddle curves up, along others down, so it has no minimum:

    Minimizing a quadratic¶

    To minimize

    \[ f(\mathbf{x}) = \tfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{x}^T Q \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{c}^T\mathbf{x} \]

    with \(Q\) positive definite, set every partial derivative to zero. That gives a linear system, \(Q\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{c}\), with one solution: the bottom of the bowl in the figure.

    Least squares¶

    When \(A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}\) has no exact solution, minimize the error \(|A\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{b}|^2\) instead. This is a quadratic, and setting its derivatives to zero gives

    \[ A^TA\,\mathbf{x} = A^T\mathbf{b} \]

    whose solution is the pseudo-inverse answer from Solving Linear Systems. Adding a penalty \(\lambda^2|\mathbf{x}|^2\) that keeps \(\mathbf{x}\) small gives

    \[ (A^TA + \lambda^2 I)\,\mathbf{x} = A^T\mathbf{b} \]

    which always has a solution, even when \(A\) is singular. This damped least squares returns in Chapter 4.

    Quadratic programs¶

    A quadratic program (QP) minimizes a quadratic subject to linear constraints: equalities \(E\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{d}\) and inequalities \(G\mathbf{x} \le \mathbf{h}\). With inequalities there's no formula; solvers search for the answer iteratively. Chapter 7's whole-body controller solves a QP every control cycle.
